Leak Ledger is our weekly roundup of leaked documents, images and video. Six stories this week: deal papers, an internal note and a draft that reporters saw, two sets of data taken by hackers, and an insider case that rests on allegations.

<Roundup>
  <Item headline="Documents on FIFA's failed World Cup sell-off reach The Guardian" date="2026-11-23" source="https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/nov/23/gianni-infantino-world-cup-sell-off-plan-96-years-documents-payouts" sourceName="The Guardian">
    The Guardian saw communications between FIFA and its lead investor, a term sheet and internal FIFA documents, including a risk assessment, about the scrapped plan for a commercial subsidiary; FIFA denies Infantino would have profited. Deal papers like these usually pass between a small set of executives, advisers and investors. Had each of them received a separately watermarked file, a copy that surfaced intact could be matched to one of them; a summary or retyped extract would carry nothing.
  </Item>
  <Item headline="An internal German government note on U.S. troop cuts reaches Politico" date="2026-11-23" source="https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-us-troop-cut-pentagon-decision/" sourceName="Politico">
    Politico reported from an internal government note that sets out a meeting of senior German defense officials on how to respond to a possible U.S. pullback from Europe; a ministry spokesperson confirmed a related trip to Washington but declined to discuss its aims. An internal government note usually goes to a limited set of officials. "Seen by" doesn't say whether a reporter held the file or was only shown it; a watermark speaks only to a copy that surfaces.
  </Item>
  <Item headline="Alleged leader of a global insider trading network is extradited to the U.S." date="2026-11-24" source="https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/member-global-insider-trading-network-extradited-united-states-securities-fraud-and" sourceName="U.S. Department of Justice">
    Prosecutors say Zhi Ge helped lead a network that paid bankers and other insiders for confidential deal and earnings information, traded on it, and leaked some of it to journalists to profit from trades after publication; the charges are allegations, and he is presumed innocent. The release doesn't say the information moved as documents. Where it was passed by word of mouth or in a message, a watermark has nothing to read.
  </Item>
  <Item headline="Leaked Russian death records point to more than 490,000 soldiers killed" date="2026-11-25" source="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/russia-ukraine-war-russian-soldier-death-toll-data-leak/" sourceName="CBS News">
    CBS News reports that more than 11 million Russian government death records, produced by a hack by pro-Ukraine groups, put the military toll at 490,334 or more by the BBC's analysis, while Mediazona estimates it has likely reached around 700,000; the Kremlin says only the Defense Ministry can disclose casualty figures. Records taken in a hack weren't handed to a named recipient, so a per-recipient watermark would have nothing to say about them.
  </Item>
  <Item headline="A Commission draft on data centers and the grid reaches Euronews" date="2026-11-26" source="https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/26/eu-wants-more-data-centres-but-wont-force-new-green-commitments-leaked-draft-reveals" sourceName="Euronews">
    Euronews reported from a European Commission draft model for voluntary agreements between governments, data center operators and energy companies, which it says was developed with input from authorities in 18 member states and 14 industry associations. The more hands that shape a draft, the wider the circle. The article doesn't say who received it; separately watermarked copies would narrow that only if the file itself surfaced.
  </Item>
  <Item headline="FIFA confirms a cyberattack exposed personal and business information" date="2026-11-26" source="https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/nov/26/fifa-cyberattack-hack-personal-information" sourceName="The Guardian">
    FIFA said hackers accessed a limited set of personal and business information, after a group posted on X a screenshot of what it claimed were FIFA files; the Guardian notes ESPN's unnamed sources said the hackers got in by impersonating staff. Data taken from an organization's systems isn't a copy handed to a named recipient, so a watermark would not trace who took it.
  </Item>

## Why it matters

Three of this week's six stories involve documents likely meant for a limited group: FIFA's deal papers, a German ministry note and a Commission draft shaped by governments and industry. The other three show where a watermark can't help: millions of records and a set of FIFA data taken by hackers, and an alleged scheme in which, according to prosecutors, insiders sold what they knew.

When each recipient gets a copy with its own invisible watermark, [detection](/docs/api/detect) reads the unique forensic ID from a leaked file, and you match it to the recipient you recorded. That covers PDFs, Word and PowerPoint files, images and video. It doesn't cover audio, retyped or relayed text, records taken from a database, or information someone passes on without sharing a file, and it doesn't stop a leak. Our [guide to invisible watermarking](/resources/invisible-watermarking) covers where it works and where it doesn't.
